2. Daten, die nur auf deinem Gerät gespeichert werden

Die App speichert die folgenden Angaben ausschließlich im App-Speicher deines Geräts:

  • IPTV-Listen und Anbieter: Adressen deiner Wiedergabelisten und Zugangsdaten deines Anbieters, verschlüsselt im Schlüsselbund. Listen, die du in der App „Dateien“ auswählst, legt die App als Kopie im App-Speicher ab.
  • Einstellungen und Nutzung: Einstellungen, gespeicherte Ansichten, zuletzt gesehene Sender, Wiedergabepositionen in Aufnahmen und Filmen sowie die Sehstatistik für „★ Meistgesehen“ (Sender und Sehdauer).
  • Zwischenspeicher: Senderlogos, Programmdaten und Vorschaubilder, damit die App schneller lädt.
  • Protokoll: Abläufe und Fehlermeldungen der App, zum Beispiel Gerätemodell, iOS-Version, Adressen deiner Receiver und Sendernamen. Zugangsdaten in Internetadressen macht die App im Protokoll unkenntlich. Das Protokoll ist auf wenige Megabyte begrenzt; ältere Einträge werden überschrieben.

  • Mitschnitte und Screenshots (Pro): Wenn du sie startest, speichert die App sie in ihrem Ordner, den du in der App „Dateien“ unter „Auf meinem iPhone“ bzw. „Auf meinem iPad“ › „Enigma2 Player“ findest („Videos“ und „Screenshots“). Screenshots legt sie zusätzlich in deiner Fotomediathek ab, wenn du das erlaubst.

Diese Daten erhält der Entwickler nicht. Du kannst sie jederzeit löschen, indem du die App löschst (zum Beispiel unter Einstellungen › Allgemein › iPhone-Speicher › Enigma2 Player › „App löschen“). Dabei löscht iOS auch die Mitschnitte und Screenshots im Ordner der App – sichere sie vorher, wenn du sie behalten möchtest. Passwörter im Schlüsselbund bewahrt iOS auch nach dem Löschen der App auf; die App entfernt sie, wenn du sie erneut installierst. Willst du sie sofort löschen, entferne vorher deine Receiver und Zugänge in der App. Screenshots in deiner Fotomediathek bleiben, bis du sie selbst löschst.

2. Data stored only on your device

The app stores the following information exclusively in its private storage on your device:

  • Receivers: address, ports, name, model and user name of your receivers. Passwords are kept encrypted in the iOS keychain.
  • IPTV lists and providers: addresses of your playlists and your provider’s login details, encrypted in the keychain. Lists you choose in the Files app are stored as a copy in the app’s storage.
  • Settings and usage: settings, saved views, recently watched channels, playback positions in recordings and movies, and the viewing statistics for “★ Most watched” (channels and viewing time).
  • Cache: channel logos, programme data and preview images so that the app loads faster.
  • Log: the app’s activity and error messages, for example device model, iOS version, addresses of your receivers and channel names. Login details in web addresses are masked in the log. The log is limited to a few megabytes; older entries are overwritten.
  • Pro: whether Pro is unlocked.
  • Recordings and screenshots (Pro): when you start them, the app saves them in its folder, which you can find in the Files app under “On My iPhone” or “On My iPad” › “Enigma2 Player” (“Videos” and “Screenshots”). If you allow it, it also adds screenshots to your photo library.

The developer does not receive this data. You can delete it at any time by deleting the app (for example under Settings › General › iPhone Storage › Enigma2 Player › “Delete App”). iOS then also deletes the recordings and screenshots in the app’s folder – save them beforehand if you want to keep them. iOS keeps passwords in the keychain even after the app has been deleted; the app removes them when you install it again. To delete them right away, remove your receivers and accesses in the app first. Screenshots in your photo library remain until you delete them yourself.

3. Connections made by the app

  • Your receiver: the app connects to your receiver’s web interface (OpenWebif or the Dreambox web interface) and its streams – using the address and login details you enter. This is your own device.
  • Search in your home network: only when you tap “Search” does the app contact devices in your local network to find receivers and their names. These requests do not leave your home network.
  • IPTV lists, streams and providers: the app retrieves the lists, streams, programme data and logos that you enter or open yourself. For technical reasons, the respective server receives your IP address and, where applicable, your login details. The privacy policies of their operators apply to these servers.
  • App Store: the app uses Apple’s in-app purchase for the Pro purchase. You only enter payment details with Apple; the app only learns whether Pro has been purchased and completes the purchase with Apple. Apple’s privacy policy applies to processing by Apple: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/
  • Demo mode: the demo receiver runs entirely inside the app on your device.
  • Other apps: opening the web interface in a browser, emailing the developer and sharing the log open other apps – only when you choose to.

Independently of the app, Apple may collect crash and diagnostic data under its own terms if you have allowed this in Settings under Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements. If “Share With App Developers” is turned on there, Apple provides the developer with crash reports and usage statistics without information about you personally.

4. Contact and log by email

If you write to the developer or send them the log via “Share”, they receive your email address, your message and, where applicable, the log. Emails are received via Gmail (Google). The developer uses this information only to handle your request, does not pass it on and deletes it as soon as it is no longer needed for that purpose. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (requests about the app) or Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in answering requests).

5. iCloud Backup

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your device on a computer, iOS includes the app’s settings as well as your recordings and screenshots in the app’s folder. iOS does not transfer passwords and login details in the keychain to other devices; the app excludes IPTV lists and the log from the backup. You control the backup in iOS settings; the developer has no access to it.

6. Permissions

  • Local network: to find your receiver in your home network and connect to it. iOS asks you the first time.
  • Photos (add only): to also save screenshots to your photo library. iOS asks you at your first screenshot; the app cannot see your photos.
  • Recording in the background: if a recording continues while you leave the app (iOS 26 and later), iOS shows its progress as a Live Activity.
  • Keep the screen on: so that the screen does not switch off while you watch TV.
  • In-app purchase: for the Pro purchase through the App Store.

The app does not ask for location, contacts, camera, microphone or access to your photo library. It only opens files and folders through the Files app when you select them.

7. Sharing of data

The developer does not receive any data through the app and therefore does not pass any on. They do not sell data or use it for advertising.

8. Children

The app is not directed at children.

9. Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection (Art. 15 to 21 GDPR), as well as the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR). To exercise them, contact the developer by email. As the app does not transmit any data to the developer, this usually only concerns emails you have sent them.

Con independencia de la app, Apple puede recopilar datos de fallos y diagnóstico conforme a sus propias condiciones, si lo has permitido en Ajustes › Privacidad y seguridad › Análisis y mejoras. Si allí está activado «Compartir con desarrolladores de apps», Apple pone a disposición del desarrollador informes de fallos y estadísticas de uso sin datos que te identifiquen.

4. Contacto y registro por correo electrónico

Si escribes al desarrollador o le envías el registro mediante «Compartir», recibe tu dirección de correo electrónico, tu mensaje y, en su caso, el registro. Los correos se reciben a través de Gmail (Google). El desarrollador utiliza esta información solo para atender tu consulta, no la transmite a terceros y la elimina en cuanto deja de ser necesaria para ese fin. La base jurídica es el artículo 6, apartado 1, letra b) del RGPD (consultas sobre la app) o el artículo 6, apartado 1, letra f) del RGPD (interés legítimo en responder a las consultas).

5. Copia de seguridad de iCloud

Si usas la copia de seguridad de iCloud o haces una copia de tu dispositivo en un ordenador, iOS incluye los ajustes de la app, así como tus grabaciones y capturas de pantalla de la carpeta de la app. iOS no transfiere las contraseñas y los datos de acceso del llavero a otros dispositivos; la app excluye de la copia las listas IPTV y el registro. Tú controlas la copia de seguridad en los ajustes de iOS; el desarrollador no tiene acceso a ella.

6. Permisos

  • Red local: para encontrar tu receptor en tu red doméstica y conectarte a él. iOS te lo pregunta la primera vez.
  • Fotos (solo añadir): para guardar también las capturas de pantalla en tu fototeca. iOS te lo pregunta en la primera captura; la app no ve tus fotos.
  • Grabación en segundo plano: si una grabación continúa mientras sales de la app (a partir de iOS 26), iOS muestra su progreso como actividad en directo.
  • Mantener la pantalla encendida: para que la pantalla no se apague mientras ves la televisión.
  • Compra dentro de la app: para la compra de Pro a través del App Store.

La app no solicita acceso a la ubicación, los contactos, la cámara, el micrófono ni a tu fototeca. Solo abre archivos y carpetas a través de la app Archivos cuando tú los eliges.

7. Cesión de datos

El desarrollador no recibe datos a través de la app y, por tanto, no cede ninguno. No vende datos ni los utiliza con fines publicitarios.

8. Niños

La app no está dirigida a niños.

9. Tus derechos

Tienes derecho de acceso, rectificación, supresión, limitación del tratamiento, portabilidad de los datos y oposición (artículos 15 a 21 del RGPD), así como derecho a presentar una reclamación ante una autoridad de control de protección de datos (artículo 77 del RGPD). Para ejercerlos, escribe al desarrollador por correo electrónico. Como la app no le transmite ningún dato, esto suele afectar únicamente a los correos que le hayas enviado.

10. Cambios

Si la app cambia de forma que sea necesario adaptar esta política, encontrarás la nueva versión en la app en «Acerca de › Privacidad» y en https://zakomania.github.io/enigma2player-app/privacy-ios.html.
